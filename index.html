Local-first · source-available

One place for the work of learning.

StudyAI puts your course notes, imported documents, a scientific canvas and an AI tutor in the same workspace — so you can work through a hard concept visually instead of stitching together four different apps.

View the repository Starter issues

Why it exists

The study tools that work well are text-first, and the good ones want a subscription. StudyAI was built because the useful way to understand a hard idea is often to draw it, move it, and ask questions about it — without paying for the privilege or handing over your notes.

What is in it today

Course workspace

Lessons with notes and PDF, DOCX, PPTX and TXT documents, searchable with source references.

Scientific canvas

Vectors, forces, equations and graphs with recognition, direct manipulation and a revision-tracked scene graph.

AI tutor

Answers from your documents and your canvas together. Proposes canvas edits you review before anything changes.

Adaptive review

A review queue tied to what you are studying, with spacing driven by ts-fsrs.

Your material stays yours

StudyAI is single-user and stores data locally. The tutor can run entirely on your machine through Ollama, or through a provider you configure yourself. There is no hosted service and no multi-user account.

Why the licence matters

StudyAI is released under MIT + Commons Clause: you can read, use, modify and share the source, but you cannot sell the software. That is a deliberate choice for now, and it means the project is source-available, not OSI open source. It will not be a fit for communities that require an open-source licence — and that is fine.

Looking for people to own a piece

This is an invitation, not a roadmap. The author keeps the learning experience and the vision; the hope is to find two or three people who want to own one of these areas:

  • The visual canvas — drawing, rendering, recognition, direct manipulation.
  • The tutor and study workflows — review, learning activities, progress.
  • The document and knowledge layer — import, retrieval, source grounding.
  • Provider plumbing — routing, disclosure, budgets, benchmarks, CI.
  • Student experience — onboarding, docs, examples.

There are three small, well-described starter issues if you would rather begin there. Read the vision and say hello.
